# Whirr's Blueprint Works; Its Idle Behavior Doesn't

> **Update (2026-11-24):** Whirr now targets macOS 27 or later (`platforms: [.macOS(.v27)]`, Swift 6.4), which also means Apple Silicon only. Where this review recommends `.macOS(.v15)`, testing on macOS 15 and 26, or universal Intel builds, those points are superseded. The rest of the research stands as written.

Whirr's proposed architecture is structurally sound. Each of its five pillars matches what comparable, well-maintained macOS tools ship today, and none of them needs to be replaced. Polling IOKit's `IOBlockStorageDriver` "Statistics" is the only system-wide, unprivileged disk-I/O source on macOS, and it is the same data `iostat`, Stats and BlinkenDisk read. A per-user LaunchAgent driven by modern `launchctl` verbs is exactly how Homebrew services, yabai and skhd run. A pre-rendered AVAudioEngine buffer pool is the pattern used by native keyboard-sound apps. The weaknesses are in what the tool does between sounds, not in its structure. Three behaviours, as naively implemented, would fail in practice. A permanently running audio engine keeps coreaudiod holding a sleep-preventing power assertion. An idle Apple Silicon SSD changes its counters 190–340 times per second, so an "any change makes a click" trigger would chatter constantly. And the design has no plan for output-device switches, hot-plugged and disk-image drives, or sleep/wake. A dozen concrete refinements, listed in priority order below, fix these without adding a menu bar app or an `.app` bundle. With those changes, end-to-end latency on wired or built-in output stays around 25–45 ms. That is imperceptible for a sound tied to invisible disk activity. Bluetooth output adds roughly 145–275 ms (measured AirPods figures) that no code can remove. Distribution is simplest as a source-built formula in a personal Homebrew tap. Developer ID signing and notarization are optional unless you also offer direct browser downloads, and they are worth doing to give the Login Items entry a proper name.

## Four pillars are mainstream; the fifth is a defensible outlier

The table below gives the per-component verdict. "Precedent" names shipping projects whose source confirms the same choice. The rest of the report gives the evidence and the specific refinements.

| # | Proposed component | Verdict | Closest precedent | What must change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Single SwiftPM CLI, no Xcode project, no `.app`, no menu bar | **Sound, common for CLIs**; "lighter" mostly means less memory and no AppKit, not less CPU | xcodes, mas (Swift); yabai, skhd (C) | Embed a minimal Info.plist; set `platforms: [.macOS(.v15)]`; use `--show-bin-path`; add `mute`/`toggle` subcommands; consider Developer ID signing |
| 2 | Poll IOBlockStorageDriver "Statistics" every 20–33 ms | **Best available and standard practice** | iostat (1 s), Stats (1 s), BlinkenDisk (50 ms) | Baseline/threshold idle SSD noise; cache handles; exclude disk-image drivers; hot-plug notifications; drop first delta after wake; stop polling when muted |
| 3 | AVAudioEngine with pre-generated buffers and per-click variation | **Acceptable and common**, but incomplete without an idle stop | Clacky (AVAudioEngine); Thock (AudioQueue, closer in spirit) | Lazy start plus pause after ~10 s silence; 256-frame IO buffer; fixed pre-connected player pool; device-change handling; verify `isRunning` |
| 4 | Per-user LaunchAgent in `~/Library/LaunchAgents`, `whirr start/stop/status/volume` | **Standard for non-bundled tools**; SMAppService needs a bundle | `brew services`, yabai, skhd | Use `bootstrap`/`bootout`/`kickstart`/`print` on `gui/$UID`; `ProcessType=Interactive`; `KeepAlive={SuccessfulExit=false}`; stable binary path; detect the Login Items "off" toggle |
| 5 | Procedurally synthesized sounds instead of recordings | **Unusual but defensible**; nearly all prior art uses samples | ClatterDrive (procedural); MAME, 86Box, DOSBox Staging, DiskClick (samples) | Pre-render a variation bank offline; never synthesize in the render callback; make the spindle hum optional because it defeats the idle stop |

It helps to steelman the menu bar alternative before accepting the "lighter than a menu bar app" premise. Whirr's ongoing cost comes from the polling loop and the audio engine, and those cost the same whether or not an AppKit status item exists. Dropping the menu bar buys a smaller memory footprint and no window-server connection. The real costs of going bare are elsewhere. Whirr loses `NSWorkspace` wake notifications (`IORegisterForSystemPower` replaces them). It has no Info.plist unless you embed one. It shows up in Login Items as a bare executable name or "unidentified developer" unless it is signed. And there is no one-click mute, which every comparable app treats as essential: Klack ships a global toggle shortcut and advertises "0% CPU when disabled", and DiskClick users explicitly asked for a pause option ([Klack App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/klack/id6446206067); [DiskClick community](https://deervo.itch.io/diskclick/community)). A `whirr mute`/`whirr toggle` subcommand that also stops polling and audio closes most of that gap while keeping the CLI-only shape.

## IOKit polling is the only unprivileged option, and idle SSD noise is the real enemy

Every block device's `IOBlockStorageDriver` publishes a "Statistics" dictionary of **64-bit cumulative counters**: bytes, operations, total time, errors and retries, each split by read and write. The kernel creates the OSNumber objects once and updates them in place **at every I/O completion, with no batching** ([IOStorageFamily IOBlockStorageDriver.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple-oss-distributions/IOStorageFamily/main/IOBlockStorageDriver.cpp)). A local test on the research machine (Apple Silicon, macOS 27.2, SIP on, ordinary user, unsandboxed Swift binary) read live counters with `IOServiceGetMatchingServices` and `IORegistryEntryCreateCFProperty`. It needed no root, no entitlement and no TCC prompt. Read counters moved **0.2–0.6 ms after `pread` returned**, so a 20–33 ms poll detects activity within one poll period. The same code path is used by Stats, Rust's `sysinfo`, gopsutil and Apple's own `iostat` ([Stats readers.swift](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/exelban/stats/master/Modules/Disk/readers.swift); [system_cmds iostat.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple-oss-distributions/system_cmds/main/iostat/iostat.c)). Every alternative fails a hard requirement. `fs_usage`/ktrace and DTrace need root, and DTrace is further restricted by SIP. Endpoint Security needs root, a restricted entitlement and Full Disk Access, and it reports file operations rather than block I/O. FSEvents misses reads and coalesces events. Per-process `proc_pid_rusage` sweeps failed with EPERM on 198 of 635 PIDs in the local test, so they are blind to the daemons that do most background I/O ([Apple ES client docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/endpointsecurity/client); [FSEventStreamCreate](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreservices/1443980-fseventstreamcreate)). Polling is unavoidable because the driver fires no IOKit notification when the counters change.

The proposed 20–33 ms interval is well chosen. The closest precedent, BlinkenDisk, polls at **50 ms** with a Foundation timer and flashes an LED on any counter change ([BlinkenDisk StatusController.swift](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davepl/BlinkenDisk/main/Sources/BlinkenDisk/StatusController.swift)). One secondary write-up claims it runs at 120 Hz, but its source code says 50 ms. Stats polls at 1 s, which suits a graph but is far too slow for sound. The measured cost is modest but not zero: about **30 µs per poll** of all drivers in a tight loop, and **0.67–0.80% of one core** for a real 30 Hz `usleep` loop. The per-poll cost is higher in the real loop, likely because of wake-up overhead on efficiency cores. Energy impact and idle-wakeup counts were not measured, so verify them with `powermetrics` before release. Two cheap savings follow. Cache the service handles, as iostat and Stats do (BlinkenDisk re-enumerates on every poll). Read the single "Statistics" key rather than all of the entry's properties. The "Latency Time" fields are always zero in the current IOStorageFamily source and on the test machine, so drive behaviour from Bytes and Operations only.

The design risk is over-triggering, not missing events. On a supposedly idle desktop, the internal SSD's counters changed **190–340 times per second** from logging, APFS metadata and system daemons (local test). Nearly every 30 Hz poll would see a non-zero delta, so a literal "any change → click" rule, BlinkenDisk's rule, would turn Whirr into constant chatter. The fix is to treat deltas as a signal against an adaptive baseline. One approach is to track a slow moving average of bytes and operations per poll during quiet periods and sound only the excess. Map operations to discrete seek clicks and bytes to the length or density of grinding chatter. Keep separate read and write voicings, and cap the click rate. These specific thresholds are a design recommendation, not something the research measured. Tune them on real machines, because the noise floor will differ between Apple Silicon, T2 Intel and external drives.

Driver selection needs care too. On the Apple Silicon test machine only two `IOBlockStorageDriver` instances existed: the internal NVMe and an empty SD slot. The synthesized APFS containers have no driver of their own, so matching the class does not double-count APFS volumes. Mounting a 20 MB `.dmg`, however, created a **third driver** (`IOHDIXHDDriveOutKernel → IODiskImageBlockStorageDeviceOutKernel → IOBlockStorageDriver`) whose writes were later written again to the backing file on disk0. Summing all drivers therefore double-counts disk-image I/O. Skip drivers whose provider chain contains the disk-image classes, and key state by registry entry ID, as Stats does. Drivers come and go at runtime with DMGs, USB disks and SD cards, and a re-attached device returns with a new registry ID and zeroed counters. Follow `iostat` here: register `IOServiceAddMatchingNotification` for first-match and terminated events instead of re-scanning ([iostat.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/apple-oss-distributions/system_cmds/main/iostat/iostat.c)). Treat a first sample or a negative delta as zero, as Stats does, and reset baselines on wake to avoid a catch-up burst. Wraparound is a non-issue, since 2^64 bytes at 7 GB/s takes about 83 years. One semantic point is worth stating in the docs: these counters sit below the unified buffer cache. Reads served from RAM make no sound, and buffered writes arrive as coalesced bursts at flush time, which arguably fits the "physical disk" metaphor.

## AVAudioEngine is fine, but only if it learns to stop

A persistent, already-running output is the right foundation. With one running, trigger-to-sound latency is bounded by the IO buffer plus device latency, because Apple's guidance is that buffer size and latency have "a one to one relationship" ([Apple TN2321](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/technotes/tn2321/_index.html)). Per-trigger APIs such as `afplay`, NSSound, AVAudioPlayer and AudioServices add setup and warm-up costs ranging from tens to hundreds of milliseconds, and are unsuitable at disk-event rates. Native keyboard-sound apps show the two workable designs. **Clacky** runs one AVAudioEngine with **16 pre-connected AVAudioPlayerNodes** that start once, and each trigger only calls `scheduleBuffer(..., options: [.interrupts])` on a round-robin voice. It never calls `stop()`/`play()` per trigger, because that forces a stop/restart cycle ([Clacky AudioEngine.swift](https://github.com/jasonzh0/Clacky/blob/main/Clacky/Core/AudioEngine.swift)). **Thock**, the most mature open-source example (about 1,000 stars, active November 2026), uses an AudioQueue with **256-frame buffers**, mixes pre-loaded PCM itself, and claims sub-10 ms latency ([Thock SoundManager.swift](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/blob/main/Thock/Managers/SoundManager.swift)). Whirr's AVAudioEngine plan matches Clacky's approach and is acceptable. Its main appeal for a Swift-only codebase is that the real-time-safety burden stays inside Apple's code. Your code calls `scheduleBuffer` from an ordinary thread and never writes a render callback that has to avoid ARC, allocation and locks ([WWDC19 session 510](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/510/); [Swift Forums: realtime threads](https://forums.swift.org/t/realtime-threads-with-swift/40562)).

What Clacky lacks, and Whirr cannot do without, is an idle stop. Apple states plainly that stopping player nodes "doesn't pause or stop the engine or the underlying hardware", and that apps should pause or stop the engine when no audio is needed "to minimize power consumption" ([AVAudioEngine docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudioengine.md)). While any client's output runs, **coreaudiod holds a `PreventUserIdleSystemSleep` assertion** in that client's name. Users routinely trace "my Mac won't sleep" to apps that leave audio open, and the client cannot lower that assertion ([MacRumors coreaudiod thread](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/mac-studio-sleep-issue-coreaudiod-spamming-my-log-with-released-preventuseridlesystemsleep.2456169); [Apple Dev Forums 130042](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/130042)). A running engine also wakes the CPU every IO cycle, about 188 times per second at 256 frames and 48 kHz, even when it renders silence. Thock's answer is the template: **stop after 10 s without sound, restart on the next event, and play the triggering sound late rather than dropping it** ([Thock SettingsManager.swift](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/blob/main/Thock/Managers/SettingsManager.swift)). For AVAudioEngine, use `pause()` rather than `stop()` so prepared resources survive. Never tear down the graph on idle: one report puts `AudioComponentInstanceNew` at 2–3 s on some Macs ([Apple Dev Forums 756760](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/756760)). Whether `pause()` releases coreaudiod's sleep assertion on macOS 26/27 is unverified, so check it with `pmset -g assertions` and fall back to `stop()` if it does not.

macOS has no `AVAudioSession`, so set the buffer size yourself: apply `kAudioDevicePropertyBufferFrameSize` (256 frames, about 5.3 ms at 48 kHz) to `engine.outputNode.audioUnit` within the device's `BufferFrameSizeRange`. Do not adopt the "Favor Saving Power" hint, which pushes the buffer to 4,096 frames (about 85 ms) ([Apple TN2321](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/technotes/tn2321/_index.html)). The small buffer's power cost only lasts as long as the engine runs, which is a second reason the idle stop matters. Device changes are the most common source of bugs in this category. Klack's changelog is full of fixes for freezes and hangs around output handling ([Klack App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/klack/id6446206067)). When the sample rate or channel count changes, AVAudioEngine "stops, uninitializes itself" and posts `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange` ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/foundation/nsnotification/name-swift.struct/avaudioengineconfigurationchange.md)). Developers report having to re-attach and reconnect player nodes, or rebuild the engine entirely, after AirPods route switches ([Apple Dev Forums 772380](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/772380)). `start()` can also return success while the engine immediately stops again, so check `isRunning` ([Apple Dev Forums 825253](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/825253)). The robust signal is the one Thock uses: HAL property listeners on `kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultOutputDevice` and `kAudioHardwarePropertyDevices` ([Thock AudioDeviceManager.swift](https://github.com/kamillobinski/thock/blob/main/Thock/Managers/AudioDeviceManager.swift)). Combine that with the configuration-change notification, dispatch to your own queue, and if the engine is idle simply mark it dirty and rebuild lazily on the next event. Pre-render the bank at a fixed 48 kHz and let the mixer resample, so a rate change never forces re-synthesis.

The latency budget below shows why the design meets "no perceptible delay" on wired or built-in output and cannot meet it on Bluetooth.

| Stage | Wired / built-in, engine running | After idle stop | Bluetooth (AirPods) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Poll detection (20–33 ms period) | ~10–17 ms mean, ≤33 ms worst | same | same |
| Counter lag after I/O completes | <1 ms (local test) | same | same |
| IO buffer (256 frames @ 48 kHz) + scheduling jitter | ~5–11 ms | same | same |
| Device/safety-offset latency | unmeasured; query `kAudioDevicePropertyLatency` | same | — |
| Engine restart | 0 | tens of ms if graph kept (unquantified on macOS) | same |
| Wireless transport | 0 | 0 | **~144–274 ms** ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2019/12/23/airpods-pro-bluetooth-latency/)) |
| **Approximate total** | **~25–45 ms** | **+ restart cost on first click** | **~170–320 ms** |

Prior art supports the conclusion that this is good enough. Users cannot feel disk I/O the way they feel a keypress, so tens of milliseconds pass unnoticed, while the Bluetooth figures are a known limitation of the whole category ([9to5Mac AirPods Pro 2 test](https://9to5mac.com/2022/11/15/airpods-pro-2-latency-test/)). Detect Bluetooth through `kAudioDevicePropertyTransportType` and offer a mute-on-Bluetooth option. Finally, decide which device Whirr plays to. Rogue Amoeba argues that sound effects belong on the user's "Play sound effects through" device, `kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultSystemOutputDevice` ([Rogue Amoeba](https://weblog.rogueamoeba.com/2013/09/12/playing-sound-effects-the-right-way/)). An ambient drive soundscape is closer to content, so default to the main output and offer the alert device as an option. Output playback needs no TCC permission, which gives Whirr a clean onboarding advantage over keystroke apps that require Accessibility and Input Monitoring.

## The LaunchAgent is right; its verbs and plist keys need modernizing

A plain plist in `~/Library/LaunchAgents` is still the mainstream mechanism for unbundled tools. Apple's preferred API, SMAppService, is bundle-centric: `agent(plistName:)` requires the plist to live in "the calling app's `Contents/Library/LaunchAgents` directory", and `BundleProgram` works only for SMAppService-installed plists ([Apple: agent(plistName:)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/servicemanagement/smappservice/agent(plistname:)); [Apple: Updating helper executables](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/servicemanagement/updating-helper-executables-from-earlier-versions-of-macos)). A bare CLI cannot use it, so the proposed approach is correct. The command layer is where the design needs updating. `load`/`unload` are now listed as legacy subcommands, with `bootstrap | bootout | enable | disable` recommended in their place, and on macOS 13 and later the legacy verbs intermittently fail with "Input/output error" ([MacRumors: launchctl legacy subcommands deprecated](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/launchctl-legacy-subcommands-deprecated.2431281); [Apple Dev Forums 774570](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774570)). Homebrew's own `brew services` uses `launchctl enable gui/$UID/<label>` followed by `launchctl bootstrap gui/$UID <plist>`, stops with `bootout`, and checks status with `launchctl print` ([Homebrew services/system.rb](https://github.com/Homebrew/brew/blob/main/Library/Homebrew/services/system.rb)). yabai's self-installer is the best reference for an idempotent `whirr start`. If `print` shows the job is not bootstrapped, it runs `enable` then `bootstrap`, because a disabled service cannot be bootstrapped. Otherwise it uses `kickstart`, because bootstrapping twice is an error. Restart is `kickstart -k` and stop is `bootout` ([yabai service.h](https://github.com/koekeishiya/yabai/blob/master/src/misc/service.h)). Like brew, refuse or warn when run under `sudo` or over SSH without console ownership. Those contexts fall back to the `user/$UID` domain, which sits outside the Aqua session where Core Audio output to the user's devices is reliable.

The plist keys determine whether the latency target survives contact with launchd. Leaving `ProcessType` unset applies "light resource limits ... throttling its CPU usage and I/O bandwidth". `Background` is designed to throttle further. `Interactive` runs "with the same resource limitations as apps, that is to say, none" and is reserved for jobs whose responsiveness depends on it. launchd also coalesces job timers by default, and `LegacyTimers` opts out only for `Interactive` jobs ([launchd.plist(5) mirror](https://leancrew.com/all-this/man/man5/launchd.plist.html)). A 30 Hz poll feeding real-time audio qualifies for `Interactive`, which is the class yabai, skhd and SketchyBar use ([yabai service.h](https://github.com/koekeishiya/yabai/blob/master/src/misc/service.h); [sketchybar.rb](https://github.com/FelixKratz/homebrew-formulae/blob/master/sketchybar.rb)). Start without `LegacyTimers`, use a `DispatchSourceTimer` with a few milliseconds of leeway, and add `LegacyTimers` only if measured jitter warrants it. Avoid yabai's `Nice -20`. Negative nice values normally require root, and Apple prefers `ProcessType` over `Nice`. Use `KeepAlive = {SuccessfulExit = false}` rather than `KeepAlive = true`. With `true`, a clean exit from `whirr stop` would be respawned after the 10 s `ThrottleInterval`. With `SuccessfulExit = false`, crashes still restart. Point `ProgramArguments[0]` directly at the binary, never through `/bin/sh -c`, so Login Items attributes the job to the signed executable rather than to "sh". Under Homebrew, use the stable `opt` symlink instead of the versioned Cellar path, as Homebrew's own formulas do, so upgrades don't strand the plist ([ollama.rb](https://github.com/Homebrew/homebrew-core/blob/master/Formula/o/ollama.rb)). Set `LimitLoadToSessionType = Aqua` explicitly, and send logs to `~/Library/Logs/whirr/` rather than yabai's world-readable `/tmp`.

Since macOS 13, Background Task Management (BTM) shows a "Background Items Added" notification and gives every agent an on/off switch under System Settings → General → Login Items & Extensions. A legacy agent with no associated app is labelled with the **signing certificate's organization name**. If the binary is unsigned or only ad-hoc signed, the label is the **bare executable name from `ProgramArguments`** ([Apple: Updating helper executables](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/servicemanagement/updating-helper-executables-from-earlier-versions-of-macos); [Eclectic Light, Dec 2025](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/12/03/manage-login-and-background-items/)). macOS 26 adds a further prompt when background tasks stay active after the user quits the owning app ([Apple Platform Deployment](https://support.apple.com/guide/deployment/manage-login-items-and-background-tasks-on-mac-depdca572563/web)). That prompt is aimed at app-owned helpers, and its effect on an app-less agent is untested. `whirr status` should therefore distinguish three states. The first is "running", shown by `launchctl print` with a pid. The second is "installed but disabled", checked with `launchctl print-disabled gui/$UID` and, if it works from an unbundled binary, `SMAppService.statusForLegacyPlist(at:)` ([Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/servicemanagement/smappservice/statusforlegacyplist(at:))). The third is "not installed". When the job is disabled, `status` should tell the user exactly where to re-enable it. How the BTM toggle is enforced for legacy agents is not documented, so test this path on macOS 15, 26 and 27. Two smaller points complete this layer. `whirr volume` needs a channel to the running agent. A config file the agent watches, or a Unix socket that doubles as a single-instance lock, keeps it simple. And per-user agents are not loaded before a console login, which is the behaviour Whirr wants anyway ([StepSecurity launchd notes](https://docs.stepsecurity.io/developer-machines/installation/script/mdm-deployment/macos/launchd-troubleshooting)).

## Procedural sound breaks with prior art but fits Whirr's shape

Almost every HDD-sound project plays recordings: a continuous spindle loop plus event-triggered seek samples. MAME uses ten WAVs per floppy drive type. 86Box v6.0 added hard-disk sounds in May 2026 "based on recordings" of three 3600 RPM models. DOSBox Staging ships FLAC samples with an `off`/`seek-only`/`on` switch. DiskClick bundles WAV sets, and its download grew to about **125 MB** ([Ninerpedia: MAME floppy sound](https://ninerpedia.org/wiki/MAME_Floppy_sound_emulation); [86Box v6.0](https://86box.net/2026/05/31/86box-v6-0.html); [DOSBox Staging disk noise](https://www.dosbox-staging.org/0.83/manual/sound/disk-noise/); [DiskClick](https://deervo.itch.io/diskclick)). The notable exception is ClatterDrive. It "synthesizes drive noise procedurally" from a physical model of spindle phase and actuator motion. But it only hears I/O routed through its own WebDAV server, and its README admits the acoustics are not reference-grade ([ClatterDrive](https://github.com/alexyorke/clatterdrive)). MAME's author chose samples precisely because simulating drive resonances is hard, and found that pitch-shifting one seek sample to other rates "degrades quality" because real harmonics differ by rate ([Ninerpedia](https://ninerpedia.org/wiki/MAME_Floppy_sound_emulation)). The acoustics support a simpler target than full physical modelling. Seek noise comes from a few structural resonances excited by the actuator's current profile, plus a click when the head stops. Spindle noise is harmonically dense and noise-like ([DiskFiltration, arXiv 1608.03431](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.03431); [US 7660070](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7660070)). Clicks built from filtered noise bursts exciting a handful of damped modes are tractable to synthesize. A convincing spindle whine is not.

Procedural generation is therefore defensible for Whirr's needs. It ships no binary assets or third-party sample licenses, it makes "slight per-click variation" free, and a click-and-chatter vocabulary is the part that synthesizes well. Three refinements follow from the prior art. First, synthesize a bank offline, at startup or lazily on a utility queue: for example 32–64 seek clicks with varied modal frequencies, decay and noise seed, plus several chatter fragments. Then play them through the player-node pool with small gain and pan jitter. Do not run DSP in a render callback written in Swift. Varying the synthesis parameters rather than pitch-shifting avoids MAME's artifact. Second, a host-side tool cannot see seek distance or LBA, only aggregate counters, which puts Whirr in the same position as the LED-header hardware devices HDD Clicker and HDD Synth ([HDDSynth](https://github.com/MaverickUK/HDDSynth)). Invent plausible variety by randomizing "seek length" and scaling click density with operation counts. Third, and most important for the architecture: a continuous spindle hum, standard in every emulator, would keep the audio engine running forever and defeat the idle stop. Make the hum opt-in, as DOSBox Staging's `seek-only` mode is. Better still, turn the idle stop into a feature: real drives spin down when idle, so a short spin-down sound before pausing the engine, and a spin-up on the next burst, makes the power-saving mechanism part of the illusion. Klack's "idle mode that lets the Mac sleep normally" shows users of this category notice and value that behaviour ([Klack App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/klack/id6446206067)).

The prior-art survey also reveals Whirr's niche. The research found no native macOS tool that plays drive sounds from real system-wide I/O. DiskClick's community reports that it "Runs on macOS Sonoma but produces no disk sounds". ClatterDrive is limited to its WebDAV mount. BlinkenDisk does the detection half, but only drives an LED, and its license forbids redistributing modified versions, so copy its approach, not its code ([tinyapps.org on BlinkenDisk](https://tinyapps.org/blog/disk-activity-led-macos.html)). Users' complaints about existing tools translate into requirements: loudness normalized across sounds, a voice limit and watchdog against stuck loops (DiskClick's bad-sector sound looped until reset), and a pause control ([DiskClick community](https://deervo.itch.io/diskclick/community)).

## A source-built Homebrew tap sidesteps Gatekeeper entirely

SwiftPM with only the Command Line Tools builds this tool. A local test with `DEVELOPER_DIR` pointed at the CLT built a package importing AVFoundation, CoreAudio and IOKit, in both single-arch and universal (`--arch arm64 --arch x86_64`) release modes, and the universal binary ran natively and under Rosetta. Two toolchain changes matter. **Swift 6.4 made Swift Build the default SwiftPM engine and moved build products** (to `.build/out/Products/Release/` in the test), so scripts and formulas should use `swift build --show-bin-path` rather than hard-coding `.build/release` ([Swift Package Index blog](https://swiftpackageindex.com/blog/swift-6-4-support-and-the-move-to-swift-build); [Swift Forums](https://forums.swift.org/t/swiftpm-development-update-default-build-system-change/85548)). And x86_64 is fading: the toolchain warns that it is deprecated for a macOS 27.2 deployment target, macOS 26 is the last release supporting Intel, and Homebrew moved Intel to Tier 3 in September 2026 and drops it in September 2027 ([Workbrew on Homebrew 5.0](https://workbrew.com/blog/what-homebrew-5-0-0-means-for-your-mac-fleet)). Declare `platforms: [.macOS(.v15)]` explicitly; otherwise the deployment target follows the build machine, which here is 27.2. Ship universal binaries through the macOS 26 era, then drop Intel. Embedding a minimal Info.plist through `-sectcreate __TEXT __info_plist` worked in the local test, and `Bundle.main.bundleIdentifier` returned the embedded ID. It costs nothing and gives the binary a stable identity for any future TCC needs ([steipete: Info.plist for CLIs](https://steipete.me/posts/2025/applescript-cli-macos-complete-guide)).

Gatekeeper only checks quarantined files. Homebrew formula installs and `curl` downloads are not quarantined, while browser downloads are ([Apple Dev Forums 666452](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/666452)). A formula in a personal tap that builds from source with `swift build`, using homebrew-core's `swift package resolve --disable-sandbox` pattern, therefore never meets Gatekeeper and needs no Apple Developer Program membership ([xcodes.rb](https://github.com/Homebrew/homebrew-core/blob/HEAD/Formula/x/xcodes.rb)). Two caveats apply to that route. The linker's ad-hoc signature means Login Items shows "whirr" or an unidentified-developer label. And you must choose between Homebrew's `service do` block, which installs a `homebrew.mxcl.whirr` label, and Whirr's own `start`/`stop`. Using both creates two agents. Because the subcommands are part of the spec, follow yabai and skhd: ship self-management, omit `service do`, and have `whirr start` detect and refuse a stray brew-services plist ([skhd.rb](https://github.com/koekeishiya/homebrew-formulae/blob/master/skhd.rb)). If you also offer GitHub downloads for browsers, sign with `codesign -s "Developer ID Application" -f --timestamp -o runtime -i <id>` and notarize with `notarytool`. Note that a bare Mach-O or a zip cannot be stapled, so first launch needs an online check; a Developer ID-signed `.pkg` is the only stapleable container for a non-app CLI ([Quinn: distribution-signed code](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/701514); [Quinn: packaging](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/701581)). This matters more since Sequoia removed the Control-click override, so unsigned downloads now require a trip to System Settings ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/08/06/macos-sequoia-gatekeeper-security-change/)). Skip casks. Homebrew 5.0 deprecated `--no-quarantine`, and official casks without signing and notarization were slated for removal by September 2026 ([Homebrew docs](https://docs.brew.sh/Deprecating-Disabling-and-Removing-Casks)). homebrew-core requires a repository at least 30 days old and 75 stars, rising to 225 if you submit it yourself ([Homebrew Acceptance Policy](https://docs.brew.sh/Package-Acceptance-Policy)). The Mac App Store and Mint do not fit a launchd-managed CLI.

## Twelve refinements, in priority order

| Priority | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | **Lazy-start the audio engine; `pause()` after ~10 s of silence; never tear down the graph** | A running output holds coreaudiod's sleep assertion and wakes the CPU about 188×/s; Thock's 10 s idle stop is the proven pattern |
| P0 | **Trigger on deltas above an adaptive baseline, not on any change**; map ops→clicks, bytes→chatter; cap click rate | Idle SSD counters change 190–340×/s; naive triggering chatters constantly |
| P0 | **Handle output-device changes**: HAL listeners on default-output and device-list selectors, plus `AVAudioEngineConfigurationChange`; re-apply buffer size; check `isRunning` after `start()` | AirPods and route switches stop the engine; the leading source of bugs in Klack's changelog |
| P1 | **Modern launchctl**: `print` → `enable` + `bootstrap gui/$UID` or `kickstart`; `kickstart -k` restart; `bootout` stop; refuse under sudo/SSH | `load`/`unload` are legacy and fail intermittently; matches brew and yabai |
| P1 | **Plist keys**: `ProcessType=Interactive`, `KeepAlive={SuccessfulExit=false}`, `RunAtLoad`, `LimitLoadToSessionType=Aqua`, direct binary path via Homebrew `opt` symlink, logs in `~/Library/Logs/whirr/` | Unset/Background throttles CPU and coalesces timers; `KeepAlive=true` respawns a clean stop |
| P1 | **Exclude disk-image drivers; key by registry entry ID; use IOKit first-match/terminated notifications** | DMGs add a driver that double-counts I/O; drives appear and vanish at runtime |
| P1 | **Sleep/wake via `IORegisterForSystemPower`**: pause polling and audio on sleep; discard the first delta after wake | Avoids catch-up bursts; replaces `NSWorkspace` notifications a CLI lacks |
| P1 | **Set the IO buffer to 256 frames** on `outputNode.audioUnit`; never set the "Favor Saving Power" hint | Keeps output latency near 5 ms; the power cost only applies while active thanks to the idle stop |
| P2 | **`whirr mute`/`toggle` that stops polling and audio entirely**; `status` reports running / disabled-in-Login-Items / not installed | Matches Klack's "0% CPU when disabled"; BTM toggles otherwise fail silently |
| P2 | **Pre-render a procedural bank at 48 kHz; vary synthesis parameters, not pitch; make the spindle hum opt-in with spin-down/spin-up framing** | Keeps render work out of real-time threads; a hum defeats idle stop; MAME found pitch-shifting artificial |
| P2 | **Cache IOKit service handles; read only the "Statistics" key; poll with a `DispatchSourceTimer` with small leeway**; measure with `powermetrics` | ~0.7% core at 30 Hz is fine, but energy was not measured |
| P3 | **Build and ship hygiene**: `platforms: [.macOS(.v15)]`, `--show-bin-path`, embedded Info.plist, tap formula without `service do`, optional Developer ID + notarized `.pkg` for direct downloads; test on 15, 26 and 27 | Swift 6.4 path change; Intel sunset; nicer Login Items label |

## Conclusion

The central finding reverses the usual worry about a design like this. Latency is not where Whirr will fail: IOKit counters update within a millisecond, and a 256-frame engine adds about 5 ms. The real problem is idleness, because both of Whirr's always-on loops are naturally "too busy" when nothing interesting is happening. An unthresholded poll of an SSD that is never truly quiet turns background noise into chatter, and an engine that never stops quietly keeps the Mac awake. Every refinement that matters most follows one rule: do nothing, audibly and computationally, until real disk work happens. Pause the engine, stop polling when muted, discard the post-wake burst, and subtract the noise floor. Taken together, they make Whirr cheaper at rest than a menu bar app without giving up responsiveness.

Two open questions should be settled empirically before release, because the notes could not resolve them from sources. First, check whether `AVAudioEngine.pause()` releases coreaudiod's sleep assertion on macOS 26/27, using `pmset -g assertions`. Second, check how the Login Items "off" toggle presents to `launchctl` for an unsigned legacy agent. The research tested only macOS 27.2 on Apple Silicon, so a short validation pass on macOS 15 and 26, and on an Intel/T2 Mac if Intel support is kept, is the remaining risk to retire. The spin-down framing of the idle stop is the one place where an engineering constraint becomes a product feature, and it is worth building first.
